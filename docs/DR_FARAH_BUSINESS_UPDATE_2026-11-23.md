Dear Dr. Farah,

Over the past two days, we completed your requested Google Ads changes: the Urgent Care budget is back to **$10/day** (from $36), Traveler Care remains at **$5/day**, and all **three active campaigns** now run only **Monday–Friday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Pacific**, with no weekend ads. We verified these settings directly in Google Ads. We also established regular reporting and weekly reviews of Google’s suggestions.

**I reviewed all 26 current Google recommendations, including all 20 proposed search keywords.** Since your practice genuinely provides urgent care, I see considerable value in reaching people searching for **“urgent care near me,” “walk-in urgent care,”** or **“urgent clinic open near me.”** Those searches may reflect a real, immediate need—not irrelevant traffic. Our priority should be getting the *right patients* to the clinic, not simply reducing clicks.

Here is what I recommend, including the likely cost impact:

- **20 new keywords:** Google suggests Exact-match terms with a **$4 cost-per-click bid**. There is **no $4 fee per keyword**; charges occur when someone clicks. These keywords could help capture nearby patients needing prompt care, but many overlap. I identified **eight potentially relevant urgent-care candidates**, with only **one or two** worth considering for a controlled test after confirming your walk-in policy and existing keyword coverage. **We can keep the current $10/day budget** for a small test; clicks may use that budget faster, but adding keywords alone does not raise it.
- **Additional website links (Sitelinks):** I favor exploring links to **Urgent Care, Call the Clinic, Hours/Location and Booking** so urgent patients can get help faster. There is **no extra fee to create the links**; normal ad clicks are charged from the existing budget. I would prioritize this after confirming the landing and booking paths.
- **Improved search-ad text:** Better wording about real urgent-care services, availability, phone access and price may improve response quality. There is **no separate fee for writing the ad**; paid clicks still count against the budget. Google estimated a modest change from **31 to 33 clicks** and roughly **$7.58 more modeled ad spend** in its example; this is a forecast, not a promised outcome.
- **Increase Urgent Care budget from $10 to $110/day:** This could extend reach, but would increase the approved average daily budget **11-fold, or by $100/day**. I **do not recommend it now**. We should first establish how many actual patients the ads produce and what they contribute financially.
- **Maximize Conversions bidding:** This automates bids and could become useful once Google measures *qualified calls and actual visits*, not just generic conversions. It adds **no separate subscription fee**, but may change click prices and use more of the existing budget. **Defer for now.**
- **Performance Max:** Could later reach patients on additional Google channels, but it is a broader, less tightly focused program and may require an **additional campaign budget**. The recommendation did **not** specify a reliable price. **Defer unless we decide on a separate, carefully capped pilot.**
- **Automatically selected images:** Authentic clinic photos may help build trust, but automatic selection needs medical/brand review. No separate setup fee; ad clicks are still charged. **Hold pending approval.**

**Two improvements I previously promised are still in progress, not completed:** I am working on allowing patients to **book appointments for the same day**, which is particularly important for urgent-care inquiries. I also plan to set up a **private Telegram notification channel for the Admin team** so staff are alerted promptly when a new booking is confirmed; alerts will avoid patient and medical details and direct staff to the secure Admin system.

**What I need from you to make the next decisions:** Please confirm (1) which urgent-care patients/services you can accept without a prior appointment, your last admission time and preferred nearby service area; (2) for a typical week, how many Google Ads calls are answered, appointments booked and **patients actually seen**—including whether the two visits you mentioned really came from Ads versus Google Maps or organic search; and (3) approximate **collected revenue or average revenue from these visits**. Weekly totals are enough; no names or medical records are needed.

My recommendation is to **keep spending controlled**, prioritize trustworthy urgent-care searches and calls, fix same-day booking and staff response, then decide together whether a small keyword test or larger budget is economically justified. We will review Google recommendations and real outcomes **every week**.
