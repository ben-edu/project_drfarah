## Interpret the clinic's actual service

**Owner clarification:** The practice genuinely provides urgent-care services and many prospective patients urgently need care. Therefore generic `urgent care near me`, `urgent walk in`, `closest urgent care`, `open urgent clinic` search themes are **commercially and clinically relevant candidates**, not wasted traffic just because they are "near me". The goal is prompt, appropriate patient access and completed clinic visits, **not** avoiding all high-CPC searches. A true urgent-care user may call for immediate availability rather than book days ahead.

**Clinical limits requiring Dr. Farah's answer:** which acute conditions the clinic handles; whether unscheduled walk-in patients are accepted; whether intake depends on capacity/staff; last admission, urgent vs ER/emergency boundaries, phone escalation and nearby geography. Do not imply emergency department/24-hour availability or guaranteed immediate care. Nonemergency urgent-care ads may need to direct urgent users to **call now** while online same-day booking is unavailable. Medical treatment claims require clinic approval and compliance with Google healthcare policy. Sources:

## Interpretation of cost and historical evidence

**Proven historic service signal:** During Nov 16–20, the existing Walk-In ad group already served the search term `urgent care near me` with **$92.13 served cost / 24 clicks / 2 Google Ads conversions**; the Urgent Search campaign totaled **$216.17 / 58 clicks / 6 platform conversions**. These platform signals are NOT independently confirmed completed patients. The search term accounted for ~42.6% of campaign spend and has demonstrated some *platform*-conversion signal, so it should not be broadly negatived.

**Budget guardrail:** Owner-approved and API-verified Urgent Search **$10 average daily**; Traveler Search **$5/day**. Ad schedule all three active campaigns Mon–Fri 08:30–16:30 Pacific; Local Services separate billing unknown. A fixed daily budget is an **average**, not a guaranteed $10 hard ceiling every day; Google may overdeliver up to 2× average daily budget for most campaigns with applicable normal monthly charging limits and budget-change exceptions. See https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486637 and https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1704424 .

**Google forecast vs actual:** Its CAMPAIGN_BUDGET recommendation proposes **$110/day** (11× $10, **+$100 average/day**) and alternatives $36, $61, $159. This is not an authorization, an invoice, or a guaranteed patient outcome. For standard campaigns and a **hypothetical full month with an unchanged average daily budget**, Google monthly charging-limit references would be **$304 for $10/day versus $3,344 for $110/day**, an increase of **$3,040**. These are charging-limit illustrations **not expected monthly spend**, particularly with weekday scheduling, changes during the month, Local Services billed separately, taxes/credits and actual demand. At the current approved spend, annualized/monthly business impact must still be reconciled against completed patients and collected margin.

**$4 keyword bid:** all 20 suggested Exact keywords have **`recommendedCpcBidMicros=4000000` ($4)**; this is a suggested CPC bid, **not a $4 fixed charge per word, daily keyword fee or guaranteed actual CPC**. Adding a keyword has no separate subscription cost. If the Urgent campaign daily budget remains $10, additional ads are funded from the **same campaign budget**, but new eligible clicks can replace existing clicks and increase actual consumption of an otherwise underspent budget. Do not multiply 20×$4 to estimate daily budget. At an illustrative $4 actual CPC, $10 average daily budget represents roughly 2–3 paid clicks per budget-equivalent day; real auction outcomes vary.

Source: https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529 (Exact can match same intent), https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375416 (sitelink clicks priced like headline), https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111 (asset clicks billed).
